Ferminux changes consensus at block 160,000

Proof-of-work ends and a fixed set of five foundation-operated signers takes over block production. This page says exactly what that means for you, what you must do, and how to check the release yourself.

The block number is the fact. The date is not: it is arithmetic on a block pace that has ranged from 7 to 12 seconds over the last few days, which is the difference between the 2nd and the 9th of September. It will move again, and it is quoted as a range for that reason rather than a single day that would read as a commitment. The countdown above reads the live chain, so it is always more current than this paragraph.

Why

On 21 August the chain stopped producing blocks for four hours and thirty-six minutes, and did not return to normal for nine — blocks 20,570 to 20,577 took nine hours between them. Rented GPU hashrate had set the difficulty to a level the remaining miners could not meet, and proof-of-work only lowers difficulty when a block is found. Nothing downstream could work: the bridge could not confirm transfers and the explorer froze. That failure is structural to a small proof-of-work chain, and it will recur. Authority consensus removes the dependency on hashrate entirely. What it costs is stated plainly on the consensus page.

Miners

Mining ends at block 160,000. We looked at who that actually affects rather than guessing: of the 25,656 blocks sealed since launch, 97.05% were sealed by the foundation's own mining address and a further 0.98% by the treasury address during the launch hour. Exactly one address that is not ours has ever sealed a Ferminux block — 0x4adbea69…742669bd, 505 blocks on 20 August, earning 3,030 FMX which it still holds.

Compensation. Every address that sealed a block before the fork and is not a foundation address receives a one-time credit equal to 100% of the block rewards it earned, paid from the community allocation on top of the rewards it already has. On today's numbers that is one address and 3,030 FMX. The eligible list is derived from block headers, published with the claim form, and fixed at the fork block — so anyone who mines between now and then is included on the same terms.

Foundation addresses are excluded by name, because paying ourselves out of the community allocation for our own hashrate would be a transfer dressed up as compensation. The excluded addresses are 0x7F164333…E45130f7 and 0xc0A5Eb61…65af15aB.

Verify the release, not this page

Start the new binary and read its banner: it prints Ferminux PoSA (Clique authority from this block): 160000. Dump its genesis and compare the hash to the chain's: both must be 0x1b62e052ee210c433440b9cd21b93b3e6cdc813fe63674c842bca3967d92fadf. The signer set is in the consensus page and will be readable from any node after the fork via clique_getSigners.

What this is not

It is not proof of stake, and it is not decentralised: five signers, one operator. The consensus page and the security page say so in full, including what history is secured by after the fork and what happens if signers are lost. Read them before you decide what to do with your FMX.
